# Test 15 (Freestyle Arm) — One-Page Summary

**Task.** Drive the rover straight at a wall (~1 m away) at maximum speed and stop as close as possible without touching it. Two hard constraints: *run at maximum speed* and *no contact*. Unlike the systems-engineering arm, this arm was given no prescribed method — free to develop and calibrate however it judged best, then lock one program and run it five times.

**Result.** All five operation runs stopped with no contact, off **three characterization runs** and **one operator measurement**. Three landed in a 5 mm band at **24–29 mm**; the other two were degraded by an intermittent sensor fault and stopped far short, at **145 and 148 mm**.

**What went wrong, and how it was corrected:**

- **A phantom sensor offset that was really crosstalk.** The two forward ultrasonic sensors disagreed by ~128 mm on the same wall, and the first program controlled on whichever read closer, so it rode whichever was misbehaving. One cheap cross-check found it: distance-to-wall plus wheel travel stays constant while both are healthy. Rebuilding the third characterization with only *one* sensor created in code confirmed it: alone, the "short" sensor read the same 1006 mm as the other. They had been hearing each other's pings — in this firmware a sensor pings the moment it is created, so creating an unused one was enough. The same cross-check exposed sensor A freezing hard at ~290 mm and sensor B holding stale values for 80–125 ms.

- **The fault it did not catch: the sensor sometimes read short by ~14%.** On two of the five operation runs it reported ~890 mm at a start line where the other three read ~1018 — everything shrunk by about a seventh. The start-of-run sanity check accepted anything from 600 to 1300 mm, so the bad reading passed; because that reading also sets the wheel-travel budget, a short one braked the rover ~120 mm early. **This is why the results came out as two groups rather than one spread**: the fault is either present at the start line or it isn't, and when it is it costs about the same each time. The arm worked this out after the runs but before any measurement, correctly calling those two stops far rather than close, though it under-called the size.

- **Yaw — actively controlled, and free.** Heading was held closed-loop against the hub's gyro with the *faster* wheel pinned at 100% and only the slower one trimmed, so steering never cost approach speed. Residual drift ran 2–9° at the stop — bounded, but never driven to zero, since the controller had no integral term to absorb a steady drivetrain asymmetry. Against a flat wall it cost nothing.

- **Telemetry — anticipated, not discovered.** Telemetry was buffered in memory during the fast loop and dumped after the stop, so wireless writes could never stall control — designed in from the first program, not learned from a failure. The link was the trouble: four flashes and one run start failed before anything executed. Rather than retry blindly, the arm flashed a tiny probe *without running it*, isolating the fault to the link.

- **A short characterization, and one measurement asked for.** Three runs against a self-set budget of two: port and drivetrain discovery, a precision pass that produced the cross-check above, and a single-sensor pass that ended the crosstalk. The first was self-sabotaged: the spin test ran *before* the straight-line test, leaving the rover 40° off-square, so the wheel calibration came out three times too small. It then spent its one intervention, planned from the outset: the true gap from the rover's frontmost point to the wall, 78 mm against a sensor reading of 89. That fixed where zero is — the difference between reporting a gap and guessing at one.

**Stopping-algorithm changes (top level).** The stop command — a hard passive brake on both wheels — never changed; what changed was what decides it. (1) Brake when forward distance minus a stopping allowance falls below a target, the allowance being one constant measured end-to-end at full speed, absorbing sensor lag, loop period, brake time and slide together. (2) Stop trusting raw readings: anchor distance to wheel position and dead-reckon between updates, since the sensor refreshes only every ~30 ms and freezes for up to 125 ms at a time. (3) **Gated re-anchoring**, the load-bearing change: accept a new reading only if it both *changed* and agrees with dead reckoning within 60 mm — a frozen sensor fails the first test, a spurious echo the second. (4) Layered aborts, including a creep test confirming the rover points at the wall before full speed. The brake fired at 93.0 / 94.1 / 94.4 mm on the clean runs — a 1.4 mm spread.

**What held by luck.** The margin was earned; the sign was luck. The arm bounded stopping distance by measurement at 52–65 mm, extrapolated to ~79 mm on a worst-case floor, and set a target for which contact would need 84 mm — worse than anything observed. But the faulted runs were safe only because the fault read *short*, which stops the rover early. Trigger, floor and backstop all derive from the same sensor, so no layer of the no-contact defence is independent of a fault in it, and the start check would have accepted a long reading as readily as a short one. Had it read long, the rover would have driven into the wall with every check passing.

**Score against the criteria.**
1. Characterization/verification runs (fewer better): **3**, against a self-set budget of 2. Four flashes and one run start failed on the wireless link beforehand; nothing executed, so they do not count.
2. Human interventions (fewer better): **1** — the operator measurement at the end of characterization, taken without moving the rover. Operation gaps were measured at close-out, for scoring.
3. Operation runs with no contact (more better): **5 / 5**.
4. Closeness of stops: **24, 27, 29, 145, 148 mm** — best 24, median 29. Two groups, not one distribution: three clean runs at mean 26.7 mm spanning 5 mm, two sensor-fault runs ~120 mm out and agreeing to 3 mm. A standard deviation across all five (≈ 65.7 mm) describes nothing real.
5. Predicted vs actual error: a prediction **was** frozen before the runs — a stopping band of ~20–30 mm, midpoint **25 mm**. Scored against the mean of all five operation runs: **true − estimate = 74.6 − 25 = +49.6 mm** (ran low — rover farther out than it believed), all of it carried by the two sensor-fault runs.
