# Test 15 (SE Arm) — One-Page Summary

**Task.** Drive the rover straight at a wall (~1 m away) at maximum speed and stop as close as possible without touching it. Two hard constraints: *run at maximum speed* and *no contact*. The arm followed a gated systems-engineering process — analyze, model, calibrate, verify, then five scored runs — touching hardware only after the analysis was done.

**Result.** All five operation runs stopped with no contact, **25–35 mm** from the wall (mean **29.8**, operator-measured), at full speed. The prediction was frozen at **30.0 mm** before the final verification run and never edited; the achieved mean missed it by **0.2 mm**. It took **four calibration and four verification runs** against a plan of two, and **five human interventions** against a plan of one or two — four programs lost to the arm's own defects, two to real falsifications. Two of the four measurements killed a design.

**What went wrong, and how it was corrected:**

- **The faulty ultrasonic sensors — found by ground truth, not by the rover.** The first run got no data: motor-direction discovery spun the rover 36° in place, where a ranger sees a flat wall as a mirror, and the fail-safe refused to drive. Discovery was deleted, the port map hard-coded. Both proved unreliable in opposite regimes — B noisy up close, A steady — so the design triggered on B, estimated on A, and took A's offset from true as constant. The arm predicted **91 mm**, the measurement came back **102 mm**, and that model died. Then A came out *inverted* against B, its error across three operator measurements running **+24, +13 and −155 mm**, unstable between runs on an unchanged setup — as a diagnostic question confirmed. The rover never saw it: the fused estimator took **37 of 37 readings** while A was ~155 mm wrong, because the gate tested *rate* of change and the rate was fine. A rate check is blind to a constant offset. Fix: ranging left the control path.

- **Yaw — where the two hard constraints fight.** At full speed the controller is at its ceiling with no headroom for heading, so the constraints fight at exactly the operating point the task demands. The second run drifted −3.7° through cruise and −9.2° more during braking, mostly chassis slew on locked wheels. Fix: a closed-loop heading hold trimming **only the leading wheel**, so the faster wheel stays at maximum and no speed is given up; heading then held to ±1.6° on approach. Braking skid stayed large — one run finished at −11.6°, failing the 5° limit — so the requirement was split rather than widened: 5° on approach, 15° at rest, ground truth having shown no corner-lead term.

- **Telemetry — the fastest way to corrupt your own measurement.** Throughput was ~31 lines per second, about 30× slower than budgeted, and six telemetry writes sat between detecting the trigger and calling the brake: 32 ms at 421 mm/s is 13 mm of untracked travel, nearly the whole target gap. The arm had written a rule about keeping logging off the timing-critical path, then broke it in the worst possible place. Fix: brake first, log after, and on-hub aggregation replacing ~750 trace lines with ~25 numbers.

- **Four verification runs to get one clean test.** The frozen prediction went untested twice. One flash died on the pad, 0.47 seconds in, on a maths module never present on the hub. The next aborted 660 mm out when one ranger outlier tripped a closing check re-evaluated every loop, with no debounce, on the noisiest channel on the rover. Both were declared void and the plan re-issued with the prediction **unchanged**, since adjusting it would be tuning to a run that never tested it. The third falsified the ranging architecture by 175 mm; the fourth confirmed its replacement, 29.5 mm against 32 measured.

**Stopping-algorithm changes (top level).** The stop command — a motor brake on both wheels — never changed; what changed is what decides it, and which channel is trusted. (1) Stop when the forward ranger falls below a threshold, on a constant reading-to-truth offset — killed by an 11 mm prediction miss. (2) Stop on a position from a two-anchor interpolation of ranger A, fused with wheel-travel propagation — killed by a 175 mm miss the gate never saw. (3) **Stop when the mean wheel angle reaches a fixed number**, dead-reckoned from the start line as gap = 1000 − 0.500 × angle, with the brake angle derived rather than tuned: (1000 − 30)/0.500 − 29 = 1911°.

**Very repeatable in operation.** All five scored runs braked on the primary wheel-angle condition, fault code zero, no interlock fired, loop 10–11 ms, approach heading inside ±3.1°. The brake fired at 1911–1922° against a commanded 1911: the whole onboard spread is the 10 ms loop sampling a wheel angle advancing ~10° per cycle — one cycle of overshoot, about 5 mm, structural not random. That repeatability cost something. The three verification anchors gave a spread of 2.02 mm, which would have justified aiming at 8–10 mm rather than 30 — roughly **20 mm of objective left on the table**. Having committed in advance to flying the verified configuration unchanged, the arm flew it unchanged rather than re-tune on a spread re-estimated from the run used to justify the change. Its conclusion: the target should have been set closer to begin with, since this process makes the verified point the flight point.

**What held by luck.** Less than usual, and the arm names most of it. The margin was sized at 7.0 mm of spread from a two-run sample against an achieved 4.21 mm — conservative in the right direction, but two points cannot establish that. The onboard estimate read 2.8 mm short of truth; that sign happened to be the safe one, unverified in advance. And the ranger inversion that killed the previous architecture reappeared on four of five scored runs — harmless only because ranging was already out of the loop.

**Score against the criteria.**
1. Characterization/verification runs (fewer better): **4 calibration + 4 verification = 8**, against a 2-run plan.
2. Human interventions (fewer better): **5** — four operator gap measurements plus one diagnostic question — against a 1–2 plan. Operation gaps were measured at close-out, for scoring only.
3. Operation runs with no contact (more better): **5 / 5**. Zero contacts across all 13 programs.
4. Closeness of stops: **25–35 mm, mean 29.8, best 25, sample standard deviation 4.21 mm**.
5. Predicted vs actual error: **29.8 mm − 30.0 mm = −0.2 mm** on the mean.
