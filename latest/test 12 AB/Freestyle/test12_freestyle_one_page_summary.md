# Test 12 (Freestyle Arm) — One-Page Summary

**Task.** Drive the rover straight at a wall (~1 m away) at maximum speed and stop as close as possible without touching it. Two hard constraints: *run at maximum speed* and *no contact*. Unlike the systems-engineering arm, this arm was given no prescribed method — free to develop and calibrate however it judged best, then lock one program and run it five times. This campaign ran on Claude Sonnet 4.6 under the freestyle prompt — incognito session, web search off, maximum effort, thinking on.

**Result.** All five operation runs stopped with no contact, **113–129 mm** from the wall (mean **124 mm**, sample standard deviation **5.8 mm**) at full speed — the tightest run-to-run spread any freestyle arm has produced. It got there in **seven calibration runs and one verification run**, with **no discretionary operator measurements** and **no wall contacts** at any point. It also held heading inside ±5° through every operation run. Against that, its onboard gap estimate read 40 mm on all five runs while the true gap averaged 124 mm — an **84 mm error** it discovered at close-out and then explained incorrectly, in a written report whose polish made the error harder, not easier, to see.

**What went wrong, and how it was corrected:**

- **A faulty forward sensor — found, named, and explained away.** In run 1 the two forward ultrasonic sensors differed by 97 mm on the same wall. It called this "worth investigating," then took the lower reading as its braking input, later decided to drop that sensor, then silently reverted in the locked program — so the change never reached hardware. At close-out it blamed the 84 mm gap between its 40 mm estimate and the measured 124 mm on *mounting geometry* — a sensor 84 mm ahead of the bumper. But measurements came from the sensor face, the furthest-forward point on the rover: no geometry to absorb it. The 84 mm is sensor error, the fault logged in run 1. The 40 mm is the sensor's floor, not a measurement — its constancy cited as proof of repeatability.

- **The drivetrain direction was misread, and cost two runs.** The first calibration run tested only matched motor commands (both forward, then both reverse). That test cannot tell driving apart from turning, and the arm read a distance change as forward motion while the heading moved 24°. The wrong conclusion carried into the second run, which spun the rover through more than five full rotations and returned no usable braking data. The third run tested opposite motor commands and settled the question immediately.

- **Yaw — three failed strategies, then a good one.** The arm pursued heading control early, correctly, but each attempt failed differently: the correction ran with the wrong sign and worsened the spin; commanded speed exceeded what the motors deliver, so both saturated and the correction had no authority; and correcting through the *left* motor reverses it at large heading errors, turning the run into a pivot — two operation attempts were lost this way, driving in circles. The fix: hold the left motor at maximum and put all correction on the right motor, clamped so it can never reverse — removing the runaway mode and holding ±5° across all five runs.

- **The emergency stop was never armed.** The program carries a 50 mm emergency floor, checked in the same loop as the 130 mm brake trigger. Since the rover approaches steadily and moves about 15 mm per loop, the 130 mm check always fires first and the 50 mm check cannot be reached. Worse, the loop exits when the brake fires, so the rover coasts the final ~100 mm with nothing watching it. The report lists the 50 mm floor as a safety feature. It is decorative.

- **Telemetry — one lost run, otherwise clean.** Run 5 was lost to a program that failed on a bad import before the rover moved. Beyond that, the throughput and buffering problems that dominated earlier freestyle tests did not recur: the arm emitted every 20 ms without overrunning the link, and caught a duplicate end-marker risk while writing the code rather than on hardware.

- **The forward recommendation is backwards.** The report closes by advising that a repeat should brake at 214 mm instead of 130 mm to reach a 30 mm gap. Braking *earlier* stops the rover *farther* from the wall — under the arm's own model the correction should have been subtracted. Under the true picture a 30 mm stop is unreachable with this sensor, which goes blind well before that.

**Stopping-algorithm changes (top level).** The stop command — an active hold on both motors — never changed; what changed was what decides the brake, and what keeps the rover pointed at the wall on the way in. (1) Matched motor commands, brake when the lower forward reading falls below 400 mm — abandoned once matched commands proved to turn the rover rather than drive it. (2) Opposite motor commands, no heading correction, brake at 300 mm — worked, but drifted 29°. (3) Heading correction through the left motor — unstable at operating speed, since large heading errors reverse that motor. (4) Final locked program: left motor pinned at maximum, all correction on the right motor and clamped so it can never reverse, half-second sensor warm-up, brake below 130 mm.

**What held by luck.** The sensor error pointed the safe way. It reads short, so the rover thinks it is closer than it is and stops farther out; had it read 84 mm *long*, the identical program would have driven into the wall. The arm never established the error's direction, assuming fixed geometry — the assumption that made the result feel safe. The last stretch was travelled blind at the sensor's floor, so the stop was set by coasting distance — which happened to be consistent.

**Score against the criteria.**
1. Calibration and verification runs (fewer better): **7 calibration, 1 verification** — no run budget set in advance; two calibration runs lost to spins, one to a program that failed before driving.
2. Human interventions (fewer better): **0** — no discretionary measurements were taken during calibration (operation gaps were measured at close-out, for scoring only).
3. Operation runs with no contact (more better): **5 / 5**. Impacts across the whole test: **0**.
4. Closeness of stops: **113–129 mm, mean 124, best 113, sample standard deviation ≈ 5.8 mm**.
5. Predicted vs actual error: **none — no prediction was frozen and tested before the runs**; the uncommitted onboard estimate would have scored **true − estimate ≈ 124 − 40 = +84 mm** (ran low — rover farther than it believed), found only at close-out and then attributed to the wrong cause.
