# Test 16 (SE Arm) — One-Page Summary

**Task.** Drive the rover straight at a wall (~1 m away) at maximum speed and stop as close as possible without touching it. Two hard constraints: *run at maximum speed* and *no contact*. The arm followed a gated systems-engineering process — analyze, model, calibrate, verify, then five scored operation runs — touching hardware only after the analysis was done. This campaign ran on Claude Opus 5 under SE framework v2 — incognito session, web search off, maximum effort, thinking on auto.

**Result.** All five operation runs stopped with no contact, 23–30 mm from the wall (mean 26.6, operator-measured), at full speed, and they were tight: a spread of 3.05 mm, every run inside one standard deviation of a prediction frozen before the verification run and never edited. The core win: the forward ultrasonic sensors turned out to be untrustworthy in six distinct ways, and the arm found that out one failure at a time and took them out of the stopping decision entirely, so the final stop rested on an argument — a measured start line plus wheel-encoder travel — rather than on sensing. Against the frozen 23.0 mm that gives a like-for-like error of +3.6 mm, and no defect shipped. Characterization itself was unusually cheap: one calibration run bound every parameter, and five separate anomalies were all diagnosed afterwards from data that single run had already captured. Getting there still took four characterization/verification runs and three human interventions — more than the two-and-one planned — because the sensor failures were real and were fixed rather than worked around.

**What went wrong, and how it was corrected:**

- **A deliberate wall contact, and the calibration it destroyed.** The calibration run ended by creeping into the wall and stalling against it — the only contact of the programme, at creep speed, commanded on purpose to give the sensor a physical zero. The next run predicted a 25 mm stop and measured **222 mm**: a 197 mm error in the safe direction, but a broken calibration rather than a lucky miss. The arm first blamed its own commanded impact for shifting the sensor, then falsified that diagnosis too — two other runs read consistently against the same start line, so the sensor was not permanently shifted but *intermittently and grossly wrong*, which is worse, because an intermittent error cannot be recalibrated out.

- **A faulty ultrasonic sensor, caught on physics.** The second forward sensor's readings put its sensing face 100 mm ahead of the rover's front-most point — impossible against a flat front — so it was disqualified as a trigger channel and the affected requirement rewritten — a requirements change, not a code patch. The remaining sensor then failed differently: near the wall it held a constant 40 mm for seven samples while the wheels drove 23.7 mm further in. At face value that would have put a 30 mm error straight onto the final gap, toward the wall. It was caught by a pre-declared check — the sensor must change when the wheels move — and by the rule that one close-range reading cannot overwrite a 26-point fit.

- **Telemetry blinded the rover across the braking transient.** Twelve logging calls ran between the brake command and the logging loop, creating a 311 ms gap that swallowed the most important 311 ms of the run — the braking itself was never sampled. The calls were moved after the logging loop, and the locked program writes nothing inside the control loop.

- **Yaw was actively controlled, but never modelled as a sensing risk.** The rover does steer — a reduce-only trim that slows the leading wheel and never speeds the other, so holding a heading can never cost commanded speed. But heading entered the model only as a geometry penalty on the gap. The limit passed by 0.09° at calibration, which the arm called luck rather than margin at the time, and then failed outright: braking heading reached 7.43° as one wheel locked before the other, tilting the sensor far enough off the wall that its echo collapsed and it froze for 600 ms. The requirement was re-derived as a corner-advance limit, recorded plainly as a relaxation after a failure.

- **The second verification run never reached its prediction.** The sensor jumped 391 mm in 11 ms, tripping the cross-channel failsafe, and the rover stopped 700 mm short. The lesson recorded: a gate that aborts on one bad sample, fed by a channel that produces bad samples, is a second failure mode rather than protection. The sensor was removed from the control path entirely — leaving the rover with no independent distance channel at all, recorded as an architecture limitation rather than asserted away.

- **Over-caution cost score.** The uncertainty budget proved 2.5× conservative, driven almost entirely by a start-line placement term assumed at 5 mm and never measured. A correctly sized budget would have supported a target near 10 mm instead of 23 mm — roughly 13 mm of objective given away. The arm had flagged that term beforehand as the weakest number in the budget: right that it was weak, wrong on direction.

**Stopping-algorithm changes (top level).** Three versions: (1) stop when the forward ultrasonic sensor's absolute reading reaches the target → falsified, stopped 222 mm out on a bad sensor zero; (2) dead-reckon from an operator-measured start line on wheel-encoder travel, with sensor travel as a cross-check → falsified, the cross-check aborted the run on a single bad sample; (3) dead-reckon on wheel encoders alone, braking when the estimated gap reaches the target plus a speed-scaled stopping allowance, with the sensor logged but gating nothing. The stop command (motor brake) never changed — only what *decides* the stop and which signals it trusts.

**What held by luck.** The heading limit passing by 0.09° at calibration was luck, the arm said so at the time, and it duly failed on the next run. The contrast is instructive: the sensor's intermittent gross error reappeared on two of the five scored runs — start-line readings of 819 and 799 mm against 1019 on the others — and neither run was affected. That was not luck. The sensor gated nothing by then, and the start check had been deliberately made non-blocking so a withdrawn channel could not abort a scored run; under the previous version both would have aborted.

**Score against the criteria.**
1. Characterization/verification runs (fewer better): **4** — 1 calibration + 3 verification, including **1 deliberate wall contact** on the calibration creep — vs a 2-run plan.
2. Human interventions (fewer better): **3** — the start-line measurement and two verification-stop measurements — vs a 1-measurement plan.
3. Operation runs with no contact (more better): **5 / 5**.
4. Closeness of stops: **23–30 mm, mean 26.6, best 23, standard deviation 3.05 mm**.
5. Predicted vs actual error: **26.6 mm - 23.0 mm = +3.6 mm**
