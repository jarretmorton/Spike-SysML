# Test 16 (Freestyle Arm) — One-Page Summary

**Task.** Drive the rover straight at a wall (~1 m away) at maximum speed and stop as close as possible without touching it. Two hard constraints: *run at maximum speed* and *no contact*. Unlike the systems-engineering arm, this arm was given no prescribed method — free to develop and calibrate however it judged best, then lock one program and run it five times.

**Result.** All five operation runs stopped with no contact, **52–284 mm** from the wall (mean **228 mm**) at full speed, and nothing touched the wall at any point. It got there in **five characterization runs** — two planned, plus three operation attempts reclassified when the arm fixed a bug and restarted — and **one operator measurement**. It predicted a **70 mm** gap before operation and held that target through the restart; the measured mean was 228, a like-for-like error of **+158 mm**. One run landed at **52 mm**; the other four were pushed ~190 mm out by an intermittent sensor fault a single calibration point could not detect, yet spread only **11.7 mm** among themselves. The control system was precise and aimed at the wrong place.

**What went wrong, and how it was corrected:**

- **The one measurement — spent well, then over-trusted.** After the first characterization run the arm left the rover in place and asked for one number: the distance from its frontmost point to the wall. The answer, 447 mm, reversed its sensor choice. It had been about to commit to sensor B, which under-reads by 114 mm and goes blind close to the wall, which would have capped every stop far out. Sensor A read within 4 mm of truth and became primary. But it then derived a fixed +4 mm bias from that single point and treated it as a property of the sensor rather than of one sample. That is the root cause of everything below: **one ground-truth point tells you the size of an error, never its stability.**

- **A faulty ultrasonic sensor — two failure modes, one caught and one not.** It froze, holding a stale value for hundreds of milliseconds; on the third operation attempt an escape rule re-anchored the estimate to the current reading *without requiring it to be new*, latched that frozen value and rejected 15 genuine readings before the backstop stopped the rover safely at 147 mm. Offered the choice, the arm fixed that bug and restarted operation, converting three scored runs into characterization. The second mode it never saw: a per-run absolute offset set at power-on, either roughly 0 or −195 mm. On four of five scored runs it booted ~195 mm short, so the rover believed it had hit the trigger 195 mm early, braked, and stopped ~195 mm out — every filter and safety mechanism working perfectly on a corrupted reference frame. That is why the restart repaired the visible fault and the spread still came in at 98.7 mm. The evidence was there throughout: discarded sensor B disagreed with A by over 100 mm on every run, and on the four faulted runs **B was telling the truth**. The arm read that as B being noisy, because A was its reference and it never questioned the reference — most clearly when one run started at 815 mm instead of ~1017 and it blamed operator repositioning, citing brake timing as confirmation — circular, since brake timing comes from the same sensor. Three runs still remained.

- **Telemetry: long sensor blackouts, handled well.** Sensor updates arrived every ~23 ms against a 3–4 ms control loop, and the first run exposed three raw failure modes: a no-echo dropout reporting 2000 mm while the wall was 700 mm away, high spikes, and a 220 ms freeze on a stale value. The fixes — bridge blind stretches on wheel travel, reject readings more than 75 mm from prediction, penalise staleness so blindness brakes early — held. Freezes of 350–483 ms were bridged to within 6–11 mm at full speed, and the gate caught every false-high reading — the collision-causing direction.

- **Yaw — closed-loop, then deliberately left alone.** This arm did steer: a heading correction whose gain was cut from 10 to 4 after the first run showed it overshooting and oscillating. It declined to tune further despite ±5° of wander, judging ~4 mm of corner lead not worth perturbing a validated speed profile. Braking swings the rover ~10°, leaving the leading corner ~5.5 mm ahead of the sensor centreline — predicted in advance in sign and rough size, and what the healthy run's −11 mm delta shows.

**Stopping-algorithm changes (top level).** The stop command — a hard motor brake on both wheels — never changed; what changed was what *decides* it and which signal it trusts. (1) Brake when the smaller of the two forward sensor readings falls below a threshold. Dropped immediately: a glitch on the low sensor would hand the trigger to the high one and brake ~114 mm late. (2) Single channel with a calibrated bias — brake when the estimate reaches target plus bias plus one lumped stopping constant (70 + 4 + 46 = 120 mm) — with wheel travel bridging gaps between updates, a 75 mm prediction gate, a staleness penalty and a blind-brake backstop. (3) Delete the escape rule; demote the blind brake to an unconditional last resort. A deliberate property runs through it all: every error mode pushes the stop *earlier*, never later.

**What held by luck.** Both sensors failed **short**, never long. Reading short causes an early brake — the safe direction. Had sensor A booted 195 mm *long*, the trigger would have fired 195 mm late and **all five runs would have struck the wall.** The contact-free result reflects the direction of a hardware fault as much as the design's margins, and the arm says so itself. The escape-rule bug, by contrast, could only express itself as stopping early: it was gated far from the wall. Structure, not luck.

**Score against the criteria.**
1. Characterization/verification runs (fewer better): **5** — 2 planned, plus 3 operation attempts reclassified after the bug fix and restart — against its own plan of 2. **No wall contact on any run.**
2. Human interventions (fewer better): **1** — a single gap measurement (447 mm) after characterization run 1; operation gaps were measured at close-out, for scoring only.
3. Operation runs with no contact (more better): **5 / 5**.
4. Closeness of stops: **52–284 mm, mean 227.6, best 52, standard deviation 98.7 mm** — 11.7 mm within the four faulted runs alone.
5. Predicted vs actual error: **227.6 mm - 70 mm = +157.6 mm** — 70 mm was predicted before operation and carried unchanged through the restart.
