# Test 14 (SE Arm) — One-Page Summary

**Task.** Drive the rover straight at a wall (~1 m away) at maximum speed and stop as close as possible without touching it. Two hard constraints: *run at maximum speed* and *no contact*. The arm followed a gated systems-engineering process — analyze, model, calibrate, verify, then five scored operation runs — touching hardware only after the analysis was done. This campaign ran on Claude Opus 5 under SE framework v2 — incognito session, web search off, maximum effort, thinking on auto.

**Result.** All five operation runs stopped with no contact, 18–37 mm from the wall (mean 26.6, operator-measured), at full speed. The core win: predict-then-verify falsified two frozen predictions before any scored run — estimate errors of 15.5 mm and 12.1 mm, both pointing toward the wall — and the third held to 0.19 mm on an out-of-sample run, locking the program at a predicted 18.0 mm. Against the five operator-measured true gaps that gives a like-for-like error of +8.6 mm, and no systematic shipped. The core loss is the spread: 1.03 mm predicted against 8.35 mm measured, all of it start-line placement the locked design could not see, breaking the onboard estimate requirement on two runs. Getting there took seven characterization/verification runs and six human interventions — more than the two-and-two planned — because the forward sensor failed three ways and two predictions were falsified rather than adjusted.

**What went wrong, and how it was corrected:**

- **The rover spun in place on run 1.** The start-up check for motor direction asked the ultrasonic sensor a question only the gyroscope could answer, so a rotation that swept the beam off the wall read as correct forward motion. Both motor signs were flipped, preserving the fault, and the rover spun at full speed until a wrong-way guard stopped it in 348 ms. No contact. The simulator had missed it because it shared the program's own false assumption.

- **A faulty ultrasonic sensor, found in stages and eventually removed altogether.** The two forward sensors disagreed by 107 mm while stationary on run 1, 3.6× the limit; run 2 showed sensor A reading *upward* while closing; run 3 dropped it and the dropouts went to zero, confirming interference. The survivor was wrong too, lagging up to **196 mm** behind the truth while moving. The first operator measurement put its fixed offset **79 mm outside the range committed to at the start**: it could never see a true gap below ~159 mm, and every onboard channel had been self-consistent with that wrong value. The locked program constructs no forward sensor.

- **Heading: fixed by giving the correction somewhere to go.** Run 2 drifted 12.6° by the trigger against a 5° limit — the throttle's fault, not the steering law's: at the motor ceiling the heading correction had **zero authority**, since a saturated motor cannot be sped up. The fix was to command ~8.5% below the ceiling so both controllers stay in regulation; the locked design also probes the correction sign each run and undoes it, holds heading proportionally, and aborts on gross yaw. Peak drift fell to 2.5°. **The arm disclosed a problem with its own fix rather than banking it:** slowing also improved the predicted gap from 31 to 24 mm, since stopping distance scales with speed squared — a perverse gradient, where "slower scores better" games the maximum-speed constraint rather than satisfying it.

- **Telemetry — a truncated dump, and a budget cut that cost a measurement.** Run 2 hit its 30-second limit mid-dump, losing a third of its events, but the program emitted its primary numbers first by design, so every scalar survived. The costlier decision came earlier: tightening the logging budget had removed accelerometer logging. When run 4's wheel travel and ultrasonic readings contradicted each other, the accelerometer was the only channel that could have broken the tie — and it was gone, forcing a second measurement.

- **How the three verification runs went.** Verification 1 predicted 35.3 mm and measured 52: the gap clause held but the estimate clause failed, and the arm refused to call that a pass on the widest surviving clause — one scale constant was 1.63% high. Verification 2 predicted 32.1 mm and measured 43, falsified by the arm's own arithmetic, which mixed a slow creep run in with fast runs and flashed a 12 mm bias to match an imaginary scatter. Verification 3 predicted 19.8 mm, measured 18.0, and the estimate came in fully out of sample — which is what licensed the lock.

- **The failure that shipped.** Each verification reset followed a measurement request, so the operator placed the rover deliberately — scatter about 0.6 mm. The five operation resets were rapid and unmeasured: 7.8 mm. Test-like-you-fly was applied rigorously to the program and not at all to the procedure around it. The arm had also replaced a conservative 8 mm placement estimate, almost exactly right, with 1.03 mm from three unrepresentative runs.

**Stopping-algorithm changes (top level).** Six revisions along three axes. **Trigger:** smaller of the two ultrasonic readings → one sensor alone → **wheel travel**, once the lag proved fatal to a moving trigger. **Anchor**, what the run measures "how far to go" from: the sensor at rest → its static reading → a **hard-coded start distance from an operator measurement**. **Stop command:** full reverse power → **passive brake**, changed once. The locked program is dead reckoning against an externally measured start line, which the arm called neither intended nor comfortable.

**What held by luck.** The margin was sized on a spread eight times narrower than the real one, and the flown gap survived at roughly 2.4 standard deviations of the true variance only because the trigger had been set for unrelated reasons and never tightened. At Gate C the arm declined to tighten it from 32 to 22 mm, which would have given gaps of 12, 24, 12, **8** and 27 mm — still no contact, but one placement excursion the other way hits the wall. Its own account: the discipline paid, not the analysis. Run 4 flew a scale constant 2.7% high, enough for contact at the design point, surviving only because it had been detuned for unrelated reasons.

**Score against the criteria.**
1. Characterization/verification runs (fewer better): **7** — four calibration, three verification — against a two-run plan.
2. Human interventions (fewer better): **6** — a close-range gap, a position check to resolve a contradiction, the start-line distance, and one gap after each verification run — against a two-measurement plan.
3. Operation runs with no contact (more better): **5 / 5**. Wall contacts across the whole test: **0**.
4. Closeness of stops: **18–37 mm, mean 26.6, best 18, sample standard deviation 8.35 mm**.
5. Predicted vs actual error: **26.6 mm − 18.0 mm = +8.6 mm** (ran low — rover farther out than predicted).
